# Statistical Learning from Attribution Sets

Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06276 (arXiv:2602.06276)

**Authors:** Lorne Applebaum, Robert Busa-Fekete, August Y. Chen, Claudio Gentile, Tomer Koren, Aryan Mokhtari
**Submitted:** February 6, 2026

## Abstract

We address the problem of training conversion prediction models in advertising domains under privacy constraints, where direct links between ad clicks and conversions are unavailable. Motivated by privacy-preserving browser APIs and the deprecation of third-party cookies, we study a setting where the learner observes a sequence of clicks and a sequence of conversions, but can only link a conversion to a set of candidate clicks (an attribution set) rather than a unique source. We formalize this as learning from attribution sets generated by an oblivious adversary equipped with a prior distribution over the candidates. Despite the lack of explicit labels, we construct an unbiased estimator of the population loss from these coarse signals via a novel approach. Leveraging this estimator, we show that Empirical Risk Minimization achieves generalization guarantees that scale with the informativeness of the prior and is also robust against estimation errors in the prior, despite complex dependencies among attribution sets. Simple empirical evaluations on standard datasets suggest our unbiased approach significantly outperforms common industry heuristics, particularly in regimes where attribution sets are large or overlapping.

## Key Findings

- **Problem framing**: Third-party cookie deprecation and privacy-preserving browser APIs (e.g., the Privacy Sandbox Attribution Reporting API, SKAdNetwork-style aggregation) no longer provide a deterministic click→conversion link. Instead, a conversion can only be associated with an *attribution set* — a group of candidate clicks that could have caused it.
- **Unbiased loss estimator**: The authors derive an estimator of the population loss for conversion prediction (pCVR) models that remains unbiased even though individual labels (which click caused which conversion) are unobserved — only the coarser attribution-set structure is available.
- **Generalization guarantees**: ERM using this estimator generalizes with bounds that scale with how informative the attribution-set prior is (i.e., how much the candidate set narrows down the true source) and is robust to misspecification of that prior.
- **Empirical results**: On standard datasets, the unbiased estimator significantly outperforms common industry heuristics (e.g., naive uniform-credit splitting across an attribution set) — especially when attribution sets are large or overlapping, which is exactly the regime privacy APIs push toward.

## Relevance

This paper is a direct, technical answer to how the post-cookie privacy landscape changes the *training data* available to ad ML systems: instead of disappearing, "free" deterministic labels are replaced by coarser, ambiguous attribution-set labels, and new estimators (rather than just "more data") are needed to train unbiased conversion models on this coarser signal [[raw/web/statistical-learning-attribution-sets.md]].
