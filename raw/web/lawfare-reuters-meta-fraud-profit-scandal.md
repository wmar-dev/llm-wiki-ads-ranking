# Reuters Blows Lid on Meta's Fraud Profit Scandal — Lawfare

**Source:** Lawfare Media (legal/policy analysis publication)
**URL:** https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/reuters-blows-lid-on-meta's-fraud-profit-scandal
**Date:** Analysis of a late-2025 Reuters investigative series, based on internal
Meta documents

## Summary (fetched)

Lawfare's analysis synthesizes a Reuters investigative series based on cached
internal Meta documents (memos, projections, and risk assessments) describing
how Meta's own staff sized and managed the scam-ad problem on its platforms.

### Headline Revenue Figures

- Meta **internally projected ~$16 billion of 2024 revenue (~10% of total
  annual revenue)** would come from ads for scams and banned goods.
- Meta generated **$3.5 billion every six months** from ads its own legal team
  had flagged as carrying "higher legal risk."
- A separate strand of the investigation found Meta earned roughly **$18
  billion in 2024 ad revenue from China** (~10% of global ad revenue), with
  internal documents estimating about **$3 billion of that** tied to scams,
  illegal gambling, pornography, and other prohibited activity. Meta
  internally labeled China its top "scam exporting nation," responsible for
  ~25% of all scam/banned-product ads globally.

### How the Estimates Were Derived

- Based on **cached internal Meta documents** reviewed by Reuters (memos,
  internal risk models, projections to leadership).
- Meta's own safety staff estimated the company's platforms were **"involved"
  in about one-third of all successful scams in the United States**.

### Enforcement Economics / "Penalty" System

- Meta's automated systems reportedly only banned an advertiser when
  **95% confident** the advertiser was committing fraud.
- Below that confidence threshold, rather than removing the advertiser, Meta
  charged **higher ad rates as a "penalty"** — continuing to run the ads (and
  earn revenue) instead of blocking them.
- Internal estimates suggested Meta would only be willing to sacrifice about
  **$135 million (≈0.15% of revenue)** to address suspicious advertisers —
  judged by Reuters' sources as small relative to the scale of the problem.
- Leadership reportedly weighed scam-ad revenue against potential regulatory
  penalties, concluding that the revenue would "almost certainly exceed" any
  settlement costs.

### Regulatory "Playbook" Allegation

- Reuters reported Meta developed an internal playbook aimed at managing/
  deflecting regulatory pressure to crack down on scam ads, including
  resistance to universal advertiser-identity-verification proposals out of
  concern they would reduce ad revenue.

### Meta's Response

- Meta has publicly characterized the documents underlying the Reuters
  reporting as presenting "a selective view that distorts Meta's approach to
  fraud," and said the original 10%/$16B figure was a rough internal estimate,
  with later audits finding many flagged ads did not actually violate its
  terms.
