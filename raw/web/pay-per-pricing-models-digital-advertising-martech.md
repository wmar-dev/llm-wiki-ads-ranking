# Pay Per What? Choosing Pricing Models in Digital Advertising

**Source URL:** https://martech.org/pay-per-pricing-models-digital-advertising/

**Source type:** blog_post

---

## Historical Origins

**CPM (Cost Per Mille):** Inherited from traditional advertising, this model
became widespread starting around 1995 when adopted by Netscape and Infoseek.
DoubleClick's 1996 launch popularized its use in display media, making CPM the
standard for that channel.

**CPC (Cost Per Click):** Goto.com pioneered the precursor to CPC pricing in
1998. Google formally adopted the pricing model in 2002, and CPC has been the
standard pricing model for paid search ever since, though it's also common in
display advertising.

**Newer models:** The proliferation of digital channels enabled additional
pricing options: CPV (cost-per-view) for video, CPE (cost-per-engagement) and
CPF (cost-per-follower) for social media, CPI (cost-per-install) for mobile,
and CPA/CPL models for conversion-focused campaigns.

## Why Search Uses CPC, Display Uses CPM

This is explained through **predictability and risk**. Clicks "happen
frequently, immediately, and on site," making them relatively easy to predict.
Conversions, by contrast, are "difficult to predict because they happen less
often, after more touchpoints, and are dependent on many different factors."

This predictability difference creates a risk hierarchy: CPM is lowest-risk
for the publisher/platform, followed by CPC, then CPA — the most risky for the
publisher because conversion prediction is unreliable.

## Risk Allocation Between Parties

Publishers typically employ a tiered preference strategy: sell as much
inventory as possible on a CPM (preferably premium) basis, then any remnant
inventory on a CPC basis, and move to CPA (affiliate) sales as a last resort.

In auction environments, sophisticated platforms like Google and Facebook
adjust pricing to account for risk. When CPM and CPC ads compete, the
CPM-priced ad receives preference as the less risky option for the platform.
Similarly, CPC beats CPA.

The mechanism works through **eCPM (effective cost-per-impression)**
calculations that convert all bid types to a comparable metric:

- eCPM = CPC x Predicted CTR x 1,000
- eCPM = CPA x Predicted Conversion Rate x Predicted CTR x 1,000

## Strategic Guidance: The Effort-Efficiency-Scale Tradeoff

Three decision factors:

1. **Effort** — time and knowledge required for optimization
2. **Efficiency** — ROI for campaign objectives
3. **Scale** — volume obtainable for the objective

Models create inverse tradeoffs: CPM/CPV require minimal optimization effort
and offer maximum scale but demand less guaranteed efficiency. Conversely, CPA
delivers the highest efficiency but requires greater effort and provides
limited scale, since publishers restrict ad placement to high-conversion-
probability spots.

**When to use each model:**
- **CPM/CPC:** when scalability and reach matter most
- **CPA/CPL:** when efficiency and ROI matter most, accepting limited scale
- **Facebook's oCPM:** a hybrid offering "scaling capabilities close to CPM
  since it does not promise any given level of performance, but only requires
  effort on the order of CPC/CPE campaigns"

Advertisers should select the combination most suitable for their campaign
goals and resource availability.
