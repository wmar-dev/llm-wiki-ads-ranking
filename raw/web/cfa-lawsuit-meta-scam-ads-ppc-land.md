# Consumer Group Sues Meta Over Scam Ads That Fund Billions in Revenue — PPC Land

**Source:** PPC Land (digital-advertising industry news)
**URL:** https://ppc.land/consumer-group-sues-meta-over-scam-ads-that-fund-billions-in-revenue/
**Date:** April 2026 (covering an April 21, 2026 lawsuit filing)

## Summary (fetched)

### Who Is Suing

The Consumer Federation of America (CFA), a nonprofit association of 200+
member organizations founded in 1968, filed a class-action lawsuit on April
21, 2026 on behalf of District of Columbia Facebook users, in the Superior
Court of the District of Columbia. Filed by Tycko & Zavareei LLP and Tech
Justice Law.

### Core Allegations

Meta allegedly:
- Misrepresented the safety of its advertising environment via public claims
  about fraud prevention.
- Failed to disclose that it profits substantially from scam advertisements.
- Used targeting algorithms in ways that exposed vulnerable users to
  fraudulent ads.
- Adopted policies that allow scams to proliferate because they generate
  revenue.

### Key Statistics Cited in the Complaint (from Meta's own internal estimates)

- **15 billion "higher-risk" scam ads** reach users daily across Facebook and
  Instagram, per Meta's own 2024 internal estimates.
- **$16 billion** projected 2024 revenue from scam and banned-goods
  advertising (~10% of total revenue) — consistent with the Reuters reporting.
- **$7 billion/year** in annualized revenue specifically from "high risk" ad
  categories.
- Meta removed only **134 million scam ads in 2025** (per the complaint),
  despite billions of high-risk ads being shown daily.
- Meta ignored or incorrectly rejected **96% of user fraud reports**.
- Meta's platforms were involved in roughly **one-third of all successful
  scams in the US** (consistent with the Reuters/Lawfare figure).

### Enforcement-Economics Allegations

- Instead of removing suspected fraudulent advertisers, Meta allegedly charges
  "penalty bids" — premium fees that let advertisers keep running ads even
  when Meta's internal systems assess a 95% probability of scam activity.
- Small advertisers face an eight-strike policy before removal, while
  "High Value Accounts" reportedly accumulated 500+ policy violations without
  being removed.

### Remedies Sought

Actual damages; treble damages or $1,500 per violation (whichever is greater);
punitive damages; attorneys' fees; and a permanent injunction against
misleading DC residents about ad-platform safety.
