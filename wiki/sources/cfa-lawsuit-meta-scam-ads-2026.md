# Consumer Federation of America v. Meta — Scam Ads Class Action (April 2026)

**Source:** PPC Land (digital-advertising industry news), reporting on a
April 21, 2026 class-action filing in DC Superior Court

**URL:** [ppc.land/consumer-group-sues-meta-over-scam-ads-that-fund-billions-in-revenue](https://ppc.land/consumer-group-sues-meta-over-scam-ads-that-fund-billions-in-revenue/)

**Source type:** `news_article`

---

The Consumer Federation of America (CFA) filed a class action against Meta in
DC Superior Court (April 21, 2026), alleging Meta misrepresented ad-platform
safety while profiting from scam ads. The complaint cites Meta's own internal
2024 estimates: **15 billion "higher-risk" scam ads served to users daily**
across Facebook/Instagram; **$16B (~10%) projected 2024 revenue** from
scam/banned-goods ads (matches [[wiki/sources/reuters-meta-fraud-profit-investigation.md]]);
**$7B/year** annualized revenue from "high risk" ad categories alone; only
**134 million scam ads removed in 2025**; **96% of user fraud reports**
ignored or incorrectly rejected; Meta platforms involved in **~1/3 of
successful US scams**.

## Enforcement-Economics Allegations

The complaint alleges Meta uses "penalty bids" — charging suspected fraudulent
advertisers a premium to keep running ads even at 95% internal confidence of
fraud, rather than removing them. Small advertisers face an eight-strike
removal policy, while "High Value Accounts" reportedly accumulated 500+
violations without removal.

## Remedies Sought

Actual + treble damages (or $1,500/violation, whichever is greater), punitive
damages, attorneys' fees, and a permanent injunction.
