# Reuters Investigation: Meta's Fraud Profit Scandal (via Lawfare)

**Source:** Lawfare Media — legal/policy analysis of a late-2025 Reuters
investigative series based on cached internal Meta documents

**URL:** [lawfaremedia.org/article/reuters-blows-lid-on-meta's-fraud-profit-scandal](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/reuters-blows-lid-on-meta's-fraud-profit-scandal)

**Source type:** `news_article` *(legal-analysis publication summarizing a Reuters investigation; original Reuters articles are paywalled)*

---

Reuters' investigation, based on cached internal Meta documents, found Meta
internally projected **~$16 billion (~10%) of 2024 revenue** would come from
ads for scams and banned goods, and earned **$3.5B every six months** from ads
its own legal team flagged as "higher legal risk." Meta's safety staff
estimated the platforms were **"involved" in ~1/3 of all successful US
scams**. A separate strand found **~$18B of 2024 ad revenue came from China**
(~10% of global ad revenue), of which internally an estimated **~$3B** was
tied to scams/illegal gambling/pornography — Meta labeled China its top "scam
exporting nation" (~25% of global scam/banned-product ad volume).

## Enforcement Economics

Meta's automated systems reportedly only banned advertisers at **95%
confidence** of fraud; below that threshold, suspicious advertisers were
charged a **higher "penalty" rate** rather than removed. Internal estimates
put Meta's willingness to sacrifice revenue to address suspicious advertisers
at only **~$135M (~0.15% of revenue)**. Leadership reportedly weighed scam
revenue against potential regulatory penalties and judged the revenue would
"almost certainly exceed" settlement costs. Reuters also reported an internal
"playbook" for managing regulatory pressure, including resistance to
universal advertiser-identity verification.

## Meta's Response

Meta has publicly said the underlying documents "present a selective view that
distorts Meta's approach to fraud," characterizing the 10%/$16B figure as a
rough internal estimate later revised downward by audits.
