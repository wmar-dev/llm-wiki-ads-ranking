## Summary

Banner blindness is the phenomenon where web users consciously or unconsciously ignore page elements they perceive to be advertisements. First documented in 1998 by Benway and Lane, it has been replicated across three decades of eyetracking studies. The article covers triggers (ad-specific placement, ad-like visual treatment, proximity to actual ads), the hot-potato phenomenon (avoiding entire screen regions after one ad encounter), cognitive costs (longer task completion, higher frustration, poorer recall), and the counter-intuitive finding that neutral-valence banners are recognized better than emotionally charged ones because users learn to associate emotional imagery with advertising.
